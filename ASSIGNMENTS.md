# Assignments

The course centers on two related forms of philosophical practice: formulating good questions and participating in reasoned argument. 

## Eight-question portfolio

After each lecture, formulate one question that:

- arises directly from the lecture's topic or argument;
- is not already answered in the lecture;
- is clear enough for others to understand without extensive reconstruction;
- invites analysis rather than a one-word or purely factual response; and
- is likely to interest other participants.

## Question-development template

For each lecture, record:

1. **Lecture and topic**
2. **Proposed question**
3. **Connection to the lecture** — one or two sentences
4. **Why the answer is not already given** — one sentence
5. **Why the question matters** — one or two sentences

Only the question itself may be required for formal submission; the supporting fields are a drafting aid.

## Quality check

Before finalizing each question, ask:

- Is the central term unambiguous?
- Does the question rest on a questionable assumption?
- Could it be narrowed without becoming trivial?
- Does it ask for reasons, distinctions, implications, or evaluation?
- Would alternative answers generate a useful disagreement?

## Concluding discussion

Prepare to state a position, give reasons for it, respond directly to objections, revise claims when warranted, and distinguish disagreement about facts from disagreement about concepts or values.

## Assessment dimensions

The course page identifies three principal dimensions:

- the clarity, relevance, and interest of the questions;
- completion of the question portfolio; and
- the quality of participation in the concluding discussion.
