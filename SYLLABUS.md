# Syllabus

This public learning path follows the eight-lecture course outline. Consult the [complete course page](https://aowiki.nsm.buffalo.edu/index.php/Introduction_to_Philosophy_from_an_Ontological_Perspective:_Fall_2026) for authoritative dates and requirements.

## Course description

The course introduces central themes in the history of philosophy from an ontological perspective. It is designed for learners in philosophy, computer science, information science, and related fields; no prior study of philosophy or ontology is required.

## Lecture sequence

The lectures are available in the [Introduction to Philosophy playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLyngZgIl3WTjov-UhEW7N145LVBPrRYLZ).

### Lecture 1 — A brief history of ontology

Trace ontology from Aristotle through later philosophical traditions to its role in contemporary science. Ask how the subject matter and methods of ontology have changed.

### Lecture 2 — The meaning of life

Examine what kinds of entities, relations, values, and purposes might be involved in questions about life's meaning.

### Lecture 3 — The ontology of social reality

Consider institutions, roles, rules, collective acceptance, and other entities that depend upon social organization while remaining part of reality.

### Lecture 4 — Ontology leaves philosophy

Study how ontology developed into a practical discipline used beyond its philosophical origins, especially in science and information systems.

### Lecture 5 — Why computer science needs philosophy

Explore how questions about categories, identity, reference, truth, and representation arise in computing and knowledge engineering.

### Lecture 6 — The Semantic Web

Consider the ambition to make web data more structured and interoperable, and the philosophical assumptions involved in formal knowledge representation.

### Lecture 7 — Toward a standard top-level ontology

Examine why communities seek shared upper-level categories and what standardization requires philosophically and technically.

### Lecture 8 — Ontology and data integration

Analyze ontology's role in coordinating meaning across agencies, systems, datasets, and communities at large scale.

## Concluding discussion

After completing the lectures, learners use their question portfolios as the basis for a live or asynchronous structured discussion. Formal scheduling and participation requirements are maintained on the complete course page.
