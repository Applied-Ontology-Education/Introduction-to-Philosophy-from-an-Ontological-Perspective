# Introduction to Philosophy from an Ontological Perspective

An open course repository introducing central themes in philosophy through the lens of ontology. No prior background in philosophy or ontology is assumed.

**Course lead:** Barry Smith  
**Format:** Online, asynchronous lectures with a concluding discussion  
**Lecture series:** [Introduction to Philosophy playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLyngZgIl3WTjov-UhEW7N145LVBPrRYLZ)  

This repository provides a public, organization-neutral route into the course.

## What the course explores

- the history of ontology from Aristotle to contemporary science;
- the meaning of life;
- the ontology of social reality;
- ontology's development beyond philosophy;
- why computer science needs philosophy;
- the Semantic Web;
- the emergence of standardized top-level ontologies; and
- ontology in large-scale data integration.

## Learning goals

Learners should be able to:

- explain several central philosophical problems from an ontological perspective;
- analyze arguments and identify their assumptions;
- formulate clear, relevant questions not already answered by a lecture;
- distinguish ontology as a philosophical discipline from ontology as an information-science artifact;
- connect philosophical ontology with computer science and data integration; and
- participate constructively in reasoned discussion.

## Course method

Watch each of the eight lectures and formulate one substantive question prompted by it. A strong question should be clear, relevant, of general interest, and not already answered in the lecture. The questions then provide the basis for a concluding discussion.

See [ASSIGNMENTS.md](ASSIGNMENTS.md) for the question-development framework.
