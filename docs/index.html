01

What is there—and how should we think about it?

Ontology asks what exists, how entities are divided into categories, and how those categories relate. These questions begin in philosophy but now shape the ways information systems represent the world.

The course is equally accessible to learners from philosophy and from computer or information science. It connects philosophical methods with contemporary problems of representation and integration.

03

The question is the work

For each lecture, formulate one clear and consequential question that the lecture raises but does not answer.

  1. 01
    Listen closely

    Identify a claim, distinction, assumption, or implication worth pursuing.

  2. 02
    Frame the question

    Make it relevant, understandable, and open to reasoned disagreement.

  3. 03
    Test it

    Confirm that the lecture does not already supply the answer.

  4. 04
    Enter the argument

    Use selected questions as the basis for the concluding discussion.
